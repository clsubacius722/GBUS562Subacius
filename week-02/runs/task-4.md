# Step-by-Step Reasoning

**Step 1: Calculate weekly buyers for each location**

- Riverside Farmers Market: 9,000 visitors × 2% = 180 buyers/week
- Union Station concourse: 30,000 visitors × 0.5% = 150 buyers/week
- Campus library atrium: 6,000 visitors × 3% = 180 buyers/week

**Step 2: Calculate weekly revenue (buyers × average sale)**

- Riverside: 180 × $14 = $2,520/week
- Union Station: 150 × $6 = $900/week
- Campus library: 180 × $5 = $900/week

**Step 3: Calculate weekly profit (revenue minus rent)**

- Riverside: $2,520 − $900 = $1,620/week
- Union Station: $900 − $2,000 = −$1,100/week (a loss)
- Campus library: $900 − $600 = $300/week

**Step 4: Check the 4-week rent budget ($5,000 total)**

- Riverside: $900 × 4 = $3,600 → fits within budget
- Union Station: $2,000 × 4 = $8,000 → exceeds budget entirely, and it's unprofitable anyway
- Campus library: $600 × 4 = $2,400 → fits within budget

**Step 5: Compare total 4-week profit for the viable options**

- Riverside: $1,620 × 4 = $6,480 profit, using $3,600 of the $5,000 budget
- Campus library: $300 × 4 = $1,200 profit, using $2,400 of the $5,000 budget
- Union Station: disqualified (over budget and loses money)

**Step 6: Conclusion**

Riverside Farmers Market generates over 5x the profit of Campus library atrium, comfortably fits within the $5,000 rent budget, and leaves room to spare. It's the clear choice both financially and operationally — plus, a farmers market crowd aligns well with Wildframe's audience of environmentally conscious professionals who already shop that way.

Recommendation: Riverside Farmers Market
